# Known limits and roadmap

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

- Only Avid Mach4 output has run on a real machine. Every other controller's output follows its documentation (and, for Centroid, Avid's EX posts) and should be treated as unverified: [run the acceptance test](controllers.md#trying-a-new-controller-safely) in check mode where there is one, then air-cut.
- Run-time estimates use each controller's planner model and look-ahead, with acceleration from your controller's settings when you import them and labelled class defaults otherwise. They haven't been timed against a real machine yet.
- 3D finishing's waterline, steep-and-shallow, pencil and cross-hatch passes ([how they work](using-nativecam.md#toolpaths-and-features)) haven't been cut on a real machine yet. The steep-and-shallow split reads the slope of the cutter-location surface, which for a ball nose is the model's own slope and for other bits only close to it. Pencil cleanup needs a ball nose or tapered ball nose and makes one pass per crease (no parallel passes either side of it); on its own it doesn't look at what an earlier, larger ball left (a [rest pass](using-nativecam.md#3d-rest-finishing) with its pencil option does). Waterline loops are always climb milled. Links keep the tool centre inside the boundary, so rows that end on the boundary still link along it rather than on an arc. There's no constant-scallop (3D offset), spiral or radial finishing. Projects with waterline, steep-and-shallow or pencil finishing, or a cross-hatched raster, are saved as format 10, which older builds refuse to open.
- Tapered ball nose, form and dovetail bits ([how they cut](using-nativecam.md#form-and-dovetail-bits)) haven't been cut on a real machine yet. Form and dovetail bits are for Profile toolpaths only: there's no dedicated edge-profile toolpath yet (one that follows a part's edge round its outline at several offsets), and no 3D toolpaths with them. The simulator's heightmap sees each bit from below, so a dovetail's undercut shows as a straight wall. A form bit's bearing isn't modelled. Their feeds and speeds ([how they're chosen](using-nativecam.md#feeds-and-speeds-for-form-and-dovetail-bits)) come from a router-bit chart put together from published charts, not measured. Its thick end is Freud's carbide-tipped profile-bit chart and its thin end half of that, where Amana's charts run thinner. Its chip loads stop at the 3/4" row, so bigger bits take that row's chip. Its speed limits are the generic ones by diameter, not any one bit's rating. The cross-section rule (the feed cut in proportion once a form bit's cut is bigger than a slot as wide and as deep as its shank) is reasoned from the chart's 1×D basis, not measured. The chart doesn't model how a form bit's chip thins where its edge meets the wood at a slant, or a bit's carbide grade. Pass depth doesn't change it either, beyond the cross-section rule. None of these feeds has been cut on a real machine yet. The end-mill chart stops at Freud's 1/2" row: end mills up to 1" use it, and wider ones are outside every chart. Vectric form tools come across only when their names say what they are, and Fusion form mills don't come across at all. An older build can't read a Tool Library (`tools.json`) holding these shapes: it opens the starter library instead, and changing a tool there saves over yours, so export your tools (**Tool Library ▸ ⋯ ▸ Export Tools…**) before running an older build.
- EPS isn't supported. A PDF clipping mask made from live text isn't applied; convert the text to outlines first.
- No plasma (THC) support yet.
- Adaptive clearing ([how it works](using-nativecam.md#adaptive-clearing)) is for 2D pockets only: layered 3D roughing with adaptive layers comes later. It isn't available on wrapped rotary jobs, it cuts one way only (climb, or conventional, never both), so it loops back through air between cuts, and it doesn't raise the feed for its thinner chip (Checks suggests one). It hasn't been cut on a real machine yet; how Mach4 runs its many short moves and loops is unmeasured, so its run times are estimates.
- Nesting ([how to](using-nativecam.md#nesting-parts)) fills sheets one after another, up to the limit set; every sheet after the first is the same size. A part goes in another's hole only when the hole is cut right through from inside along its whole edge and everything that cuts the part inside runs first (see [Parts in holes](using-nativecam.md#parts-in-holes)); holes in parts that stay put, and holes with something of their own part inside them, aren't used. A shape goes on another sheet only by nesting (or by cutting it and pasting it onto the sheet shown, then adding it to its toolpaths again), and the sheets after the first all share one size (set in the nest sheet or Job Setup). Curved outlines are simplified for nesting and grown so they still hold the true outline, which can leave up to 0.2 mm more than the spacing between two curved parts (up to 2 mm between very detailed outlines, like lettering cut out as parts); outlines made only of straight edges (rectangles, polygons) keep the exact spacing. Parts aren't mirrored. The search is a genetic search over the order and turns with bottom-left placement (as SVGnest and Deepnest do): good, not optimal, and timed, so a faster Mac may find a better layout in the same time (`nativecam-cli nest --evaluations` gives the same layout every time). How far a part's toolpaths reach outside it is measured from the toolpaths where the parts are when you open the sheet. Nesting hasn't been cut on a real machine yet.
- Rest machining ([pockets](using-nativecam.md#rest-from-earlier-toolpaths), [3D](using-nativecam.md#3d-rest-finishing)) hasn't been cut on a real machine yet. A pocket rests only from earlier pocket toolpaths (not profiles or V-carves), counts one only if it's as deep or deeper, and counts what its bit could reach, not its corner reliefs. 3D rest finishing follows earlier 3D finishing toolpaths, not 3D roughing: there's no rest roughing (a smaller end mill clearing only what a larger roughing left). With a **Model outline** boundary it sees a reference's bit without the uncut material round the model, and it finds rest areas on a grid as fine as its stepover (0.08–0.25 mm), so a crevice narrower than that in the bit's centre positions can be missed. Its raster is clipped to the rest areas; apart from the pencil option there's no pass that follows a crevice along its length. Projects with a rest pass are saved as format 17, which older builds refuse to open.
- Feeds that follow the load ([how they work](using-nativecam.md#feeds-that-follow-the-load)) are not calibrated on a machine: the rule (the chip held at the usual cut's, the extra force split evenly between a thinner chip and a higher load, a 55% floor) is reasoned from chip-load geometry, not measured, and whether Mach4 keeps its constant-velocity blending through a change of F hasn't been timed, which is why it's off unless you turn it on. Earlier toolpaths' cuts are known only to the job's material model (0.5–1 mm cells), so a light pass after another toolpath is measured roughly. Inside-arc compensation needs fitted arcs: with arc output off, curves are short lines and slow only for their engagement. Depth of cut doesn't change the feed (a pass deeper than the bit's diameter isn't derated), the spindle speed never changes with the feed, and wrapped rotary toolpaths keep their feed.
- A corner's own relief choice is tied to its place on the shape: change the shape so that corner moves (resize a rectangle, say) and the choice no longer applies, and Checks says so. Leads and corner reliefs keep clear of the parts other profile, pocket, V-carve, drill, chamfer, fluting and prism toolpaths cut, but not of 3D toolpaths' models, and on an indexed rotary job only of parts cut at the same angle.
- Rotary (4th-axis) work is indexed or wrapped ([Rotary jobs](using-nativecam.md#rotary-jobs)). Indexed: profile, pocket, drill, V-carve, chamfer, fluting and prism operations repeat at a list of angles on a round blank, or on a square bar at multiples of 90°, and the posts turn the blank with `G00 A` at safe Z for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC, grblHAL, Centroid and Buildbotics. Other G-code controllers (Grbl, FluidNC, Carbide Motion, Masso) can only run indexed jobs turned by hand, with a pause (`M0`) for each turn; ShopBot and RichAuto files can't turn the blank at all. Wrapped: profile, pocket, V-carve, drill, chamfer, fluting and prism operations and spirals wrap around a round blank (or a square bar rounded first), fed in inverse time (`G93`) for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC and grblHAL, on a driven axis only; Mach4's `G93` isn't tested on a machine yet. Rounding turns a blank round on either layout, on a driven axis. 3D rotary finishing follows a model laid along the axis on either layout, on a driven axis with `G93` (or a documented rotary feed rule), exactly with ball noses, tapered ball noses, flat end mills, bull noses, V-bits, engraving bits and drills. 3D rotary roughing clears each layer as an area on either layout, on a driven axis with `G93` (or a documented rotary feed rule); it's meant for flat and bull-nose end mills. It finds each layer's areas on a grid at its sample spacing (the tool is dropped exactly at every point of the path, and a move whose middle would dip into the model is split, but stock standing between two samples can be left for the next layer or the finish), and it follows the stock it leaves on a grid of the same kind, so a finish after it starts from that with a small margin. A finish that starts from a roughing still cuts in layers where anything it left is thicker than the pass depth, following the whole pattern. The tool always points at the axis (no ball-nose offset in Y), the reach check takes the deepest cut against the stickout rather than modelling the collet nut, and undercuts are reported but not shaded on the model. The A axis can run along X or along Y (Avid's front-mounted chuck); along Y is written only for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC and grblHAL (other G-code controllers can turn it by hand), and no along-Y job has been run on a machine yet. Run-time estimates for an axis along Y assume X and Y share their rates and accelerations, as the machine settings do (a single X/Y acceleration and rapid rate). Turn times use the machine's turn speed, or a nominal 90° a second until it's set, and 30 seconds for each turn by hand; none has been timed on a real machine. Wrapped cuts are timed by the tool tip's path. Rounding, wrapped 2.5D toolpaths and 3D rotary roughing and finishing all slow their feed where A would otherwise have to turn faster than the turn speed (and the chip-load check warns when that thins the chip below the chart); until it's set that's the nominal 90° a second, so on a slower axis the controller slows them further and they take longer than estimated. Avid's rotary hasn't been timed, so the rotary demos leave the turn speed unset and open with a chip-load warning near the axis that says it comes from the assumed speed; a Mach4 `Machine.ini` import reads the real one from the A motor. A's acceleration isn't modelled (the import shows it but doesn't use it). With a flat end mill, 3D rotary finishing's spirals and rings add a turn at the foot of each shoulder where the tip jumps by more than the pass depth from one turn or ring to the next; a smaller step, or a steep slope, can still leave up to a stepover of material at its foot. A wrapped wall cut straight along the blank shows very slightly radial in the simulation (it keeps one surface height per angle). Rotary projects are saved as format 3, format 4 when the blank is turned by hand, format 5 when designs wrap around it or it has a spiral or a rounding toolpath, format 6 when a V-carve has a start depth or is an inlay plug, format 7 with a 3D model on the blank or a 3D rotary finish, format 8 with a chamfer, fluting or prism toolpath, and format 12 with a 3D rotary roughing. Imported G-code with A words is simulated as if the part never turned, with a warning; its `G93` moves are timed by their F words. Not yet checked on Avid's rotary: which way positive A turns (along X or along Y), and whether its M6 macro in rotary mode re-zeroes Z on the axis.
- V-carve inlays ([how to](using-nativecam.md#v-carve-inlays)) are two V-carve toolpaths, a pocket and a plug, which **Add Toolpath ▸ Inlay…** makes together; they stay two toolpaths afterwards, and each offers to bring the other in line rather than following it. The plug stays where the design is, so cut the two sides one at a time. The plug is always mirrored left to right. A whole-board plug's board is the material or a board of a size you enter, centred on the design; it can't be placed anywhere else. A V-bit with a flat tip leaves narrow parts of the plug taller than the pocket's grooves; Checks warns, but the plug isn't adjusted for it. Raster flat clearing is for a V-bit clearing the flats alone: with a clearance tool, the corners it can't reach are always cleared in rings. The V-bit's clearing is still slow over large areas, raster or rings; an end mill is quicker. Projects with a plug or a V-carve start depth are saved as format 6, and with a whole-board plug as format 14; builds from before refuse to open them. Raster clearing needs no new format (an older build clears the same floor in rings). No inlay pair has been cut on a real machine yet.
- Chamfers, flutes and prisms ([how to](using-nativecam.md#chamfers-flutes-and-prisms)) haven't been cut on a real machine yet. Chamfers and prisms need a V-bit or engraving bit: there's no angled chamfer with a round-nose bit, and no **Overcut** (Vectric's offset that keeps a V-bit's tip off the bevel). A top-edge chamfer leaves the bevel uncut where shapes are closer together, or narrower, than twice its width, rather than carving the narrow part as a V-carve would. A prism's bit cuts past the outline; the area round it isn't cleared for you. Flutes follow the material top (or a start depth): they aren't projected onto a 3D model. Projects with any of them are saved as format 8, which older builds refuse to open.
- Tapping and boring cycles in imported G-code are simulated only for controllers whose documentation describes them (Avid Mach4, Centroid and LinuxCNC; see [Supported files](using-nativecam.md#supported-files)); for others, and for a cycle a controller means something else by (`G76` threads on LinuxCNC), they're skipped with a warning and their holes don't show. A back bore (`G87`) cuts under the material, which the simulation can't show, so it's carved as a hole right through, the width of the tool. Taps are cut in one pass: a `Q` word isn't read as a peck depth. The spindle reversing at the bottom of a tap, orienting for `G76` and `G87`, and a `G88` stop to retract by hand are timed at 0.5 s, 0.5 s and 10 s; on a machine that can't orient its spindle, Mach4 stops for you to do it by hand.
- Toolpath templates ([how to](using-nativecam.md#templates)) find vectors by layer or by kind, not by size (Vectric's circle-diameter filter), and there's no "create the missing layers" step: a toolpath whose layer is missing is added empty. DXF, SVG, PDF and Illustrator layers are read on import (an SVG's from Inkscape layers or the named groups straight under its root); a DXF layer that's turned off or frozen is imported like any other. A toolpath bound to a layer or kind picks up new vectors on Recalculate All, on a layer change and on import, but not when a shape is drawn with **Add Shape** until you recalculate. The job setup a template carries leaves out the material's width and height and any rotary blank (a rotary template's index angles are kept). On a rotary job the sheet's thickness is the blank's radius, so a **Through** depth there reaches past the axis, as a new profile's depth always has.
- Tiling ([how to](using-nativecam.md#jobs-bigger-than-the-bed)) hasn't been cut on a real machine yet. Registration holes are drilled for feed-through tiling only, two at a time. Where a profile breaks at a tile line the bit comes back onto the wall with a plunge or a ramp rather than a lead. Clamps are checked where they're drawn on the design, which moves with the material. Near tile lines, start depths and leads don't count on earlier toolpaths' cuts (the tiles cut in a different order there), so a toolpath can cut some air there. The run-time estimate leaves out moving the material between tiles.

Bug reports, results from your own machine and code reviews are all very welcome.
