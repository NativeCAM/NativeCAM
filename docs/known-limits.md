# Known limits and roadmap

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

- Only Avid Mach4 output has run on a real machine. Every other controller's output follows its documentation (and, for Centroid, Avid's EX posts) and should be treated as unverified: [run the acceptance test](controllers.md#trying-a-new-controller-safely) in check mode where there is one, then air-cut.
- Run-time estimates use each controller's planner model and look-ahead, with acceleration from your controller's settings when you import them and labelled class defaults otherwise. They haven't been timed against a real machine yet.
- 3D finishing uses one raster direction per toolpath; add a second at 90° for cross-hatching. No waterline or pencil passes yet.
- V-bit and bull-nose 3D finishing use the height-grid drop-cutter; the exact triangle drop-cutter covers ball and flat bits only.
- EPS isn't supported. A PDF clipping mask made from live text isn't applied; convert the text to outlines first.
- No nesting or plasma (THC) support yet.
- Rest machining covers 2.5D pockets cleared with a larger end mill in the same toolpath. A pocket can't yet take its rest from earlier toolpaths (cutting only what an earlier pocket on the same shapes left), and there's no 3D rest machining (a smaller bit after 3D roughing).
- A corner's own relief choice is tied to its place on the shape: change the shape so that corner moves (resize a rectangle, say) and the choice no longer applies, and Checks says so. Leads and corner reliefs keep clear of the parts other profile, pocket, V-carve and drill toolpaths cut, but not of 3D toolpaths' models, and on an indexed rotary job only of parts cut at the same angle.
- Rotary (4th-axis) work is indexed or wrapped ([Rotary jobs](using-nativecam.md#rotary-jobs)). Indexed: profile, pocket, drill and V-carve operations repeat at a list of angles on a round blank, or on a square bar at multiples of 90°, and the posts turn the blank with `G00 A` at safe Z for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC, grblHAL, Centroid and Buildbotics. Other G-code controllers (Grbl, FluidNC, Carbide Motion, Masso) can only run indexed jobs turned by hand, with a pause (`M0`) for each turn; ShopBot and RichAuto files can't turn the blank at all. Wrapped: profile, pocket, V-carve and drill operations and spirals wrap around a round blank (or a square bar rounded first), fed in inverse time (`G93`) for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC and grblHAL, on a driven axis only; Mach4's `G93` isn't tested on a machine yet. Rounding turns a blank round on either layout, on a driven axis. 3D rotary finishing follows a model laid along the axis on either layout, on a driven axis with `G93` (or a documented rotary feed rule), exactly with ball noses and flat end mills (other bits as flat end mills of their diameter). There's no 3D rotary roughing yet (stage R4): where the model is deeper than the pass depth, the finish cuts down in layers that follow the whole pattern, which is slow next to clearing each layer as an area; a Rounding first helps. The tool always points at the axis (no ball-nose offset in Y), the reach check takes the deepest cut against the stickout rather than modelling the collet nut, and undercuts are reported but not shaded on the model. Only an A axis along X is supported. Turn times use the machine's turn speed, or a nominal 90° a second until it's set, and 30 seconds for each turn by hand; none has been timed on a real machine. Wrapped cuts are timed by the tool tip's path; 3D rotary finishing slows its feed where A would have to turn faster than the turn speed, but rounding and wrapped 2.5D toolpaths don't yet, so near the axis they can take longer than estimated. A wrapped wall cut straight along the blank shows very slightly radial in the simulation (it keeps one surface height per angle). Rotary projects are saved as format 3, format 4 when the blank is turned by hand, format 5 when designs wrap around it or it has a spiral or a rounding toolpath, format 6 when a V-carve has a start depth or is an inlay plug, and format 7 with a 3D model on the blank or a 3D rotary finish. Imported G-code with A words is simulated as if the part never turned, with a warning; its `G93` moves are timed by their F words. Not yet checked on Avid's rotary: which way positive A turns, and whether its M6 macro in rotary mode re-zeroes Z on the axis.
- V-carve inlays ([how to](using-nativecam.md#v-carve-inlays)) are two V-carve toolpaths, a pocket and a plug, rather than one operation that makes both; the plug stays where the design is, so cut the two sides one at a time. The plug is always mirrored left to right, and its boundary is a selected vector or a margin round the design (for the whole board, draw a rectangle the size of the board). A V-bit with a flat tip leaves narrow parts of the plug taller than the pocket's grooves; Checks warns, but the plug isn't adjusted for it. When the V-bit clears flat areas itself it cuts offset rings only, which is slow over large areas. Projects with a plug or a V-carve start depth are saved as format 6, which builds from before inlays refuse to open. No inlay pair has been cut on a real machine yet.
- Tapping and boring cycles in imported G-code are simulated only for controllers whose documentation describes them (Avid Mach4, Centroid and LinuxCNC; see [Supported files](using-nativecam.md#supported-files)); for others, and for a cycle a controller means something else by (`G76` threads on LinuxCNC), they're skipped with a warning and their holes don't show. A back bore (`G87`) cuts under the material, which the simulation can't show, so it's carved as a hole right through, the width of the tool. Taps are cut in one pass: a `Q` word isn't read as a peck depth. The spindle reversing at the bottom of a tap, orienting for `G76` and `G87`, and a `G88` stop to retract by hand are timed at 0.5 s, 0.5 s and 10 s; on a machine that can't orient its spindle, Mach4 stops for you to do it by hand.

Bug reports, results from your own machine and code reviews are all very welcome.
