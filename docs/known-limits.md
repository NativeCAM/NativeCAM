# Known limits and roadmap

[← Back to the README](../README.md)

- Only Avid Mach4 output has run on a real machine. Every other controller's output follows its documentation (and, for Centroid, Avid's EX posts) and should be treated as unverified: [run the acceptance test](controllers.md#trying-a-new-controller-safely) in check mode where there is one, then air-cut.
- Run-time estimates use each controller's planner model and look-ahead, with acceleration from your controller's settings when you import them and labelled class defaults otherwise. They haven't been timed against a real machine yet.
- 3D finishing's waterline, steep-and-shallow, pencil and cross-hatch passes ([how they work](using-nativecam.md#toolpaths-and-features)) haven't been cut on a real machine yet. The steep-and-shallow split reads the slope of the cutter-location surface, which for a ball nose is the model's own slope and for other bits only close to it. Pencil cleanup needs a ball nose, makes one pass per crease (no parallel passes either side of it), and doesn't look at what an earlier, larger ball left: there's no 3D rest machining yet. Waterline loops are always climb milled. Links keep the tool centre inside the boundary, so rows that end on the boundary still link along it rather than on an arc. There's no constant-scallop (3D offset), spiral or radial finishing. Projects with waterline, steep-and-shallow or pencil finishing, or a cross-hatched raster, are saved as format 10, which older builds refuse to open.
- Tapered ball noses aren't a bit shape yet (tool libraries skip them on import), though the exact drop-cutter can already drop one.
- EPS isn't supported. A PDF clipping mask made from live text isn't applied; convert the text to outlines first.
- No nesting or plasma (THC) support yet.
- Rest machining covers 2.5D pockets cleared with a larger end mill in the same toolpath. A pocket can't yet take its rest from earlier toolpaths (cutting only what an earlier pocket on the same shapes left), and there's no 3D rest machining (a smaller bit after 3D roughing).
- Feeds that follow the load ([how they work](using-nativecam.md#feeds-that-follow-the-load)) are not calibrated on a machine: the rule (the chip held at the usual cut's, the extra force split evenly between a thinner chip and a higher load, a 55% floor) is reasoned from chip-load geometry, not measured, and whether Mach4 keeps its constant-velocity blending through a change of F hasn't been timed, which is why it's off unless you turn it on. Earlier toolpaths' cuts are known only to the job's material model (0.5–1 mm cells), so a light pass after another toolpath is measured roughly. Inside-arc compensation needs fitted arcs: with arc output off, curves are short lines and slow only for their engagement. Depth of cut doesn't change the feed (a pass deeper than the bit's diameter isn't derated), the spindle speed never changes with the feed, and wrapped rotary toolpaths keep their feed.
- A corner's own relief choice is tied to its place on the shape: change the shape so that corner moves (resize a rectangle, say) and the choice no longer applies, and Checks says so. Leads and corner reliefs keep clear of the parts other profile, pocket, V-carve, drill, chamfer, fluting and prism toolpaths cut, but not of 3D toolpaths' models, and on an indexed rotary job only of parts cut at the same angle.
- Rotary (4th-axis) work is indexed or wrapped ([Rotary jobs](using-nativecam.md#rotary-jobs)). Indexed: profile, pocket, drill, V-carve, chamfer, fluting and prism operations repeat at a list of angles on a round blank, or on a square bar at multiples of 90°, and the posts turn the blank with `G00 A` at safe Z for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC, grblHAL, Centroid and Buildbotics. Other G-code controllers (Grbl, FluidNC, Carbide Motion, Masso) can only run indexed jobs turned by hand, with a pause (`M0`) for each turn; ShopBot and RichAuto files can't turn the blank at all. Wrapped: profile, pocket, V-carve, drill, chamfer, fluting and prism operations and spirals wrap around a round blank (or a square bar rounded first), fed in inverse time (`G93`) for Avid Mach4, LinuxCNC and grblHAL, on a driven axis only; Mach4's `G93` isn't tested on a machine yet. Rounding turns a blank round on either layout, on a driven axis. 3D rotary finishing follows a model laid along the axis on either layout, on a driven axis with `G93` (or a documented rotary feed rule), exactly with ball noses, flat end mills, bull noses, V-bits, engraving bits and drills. 3D rotary roughing clears each layer as an area on either layout, on a driven axis with `G93` (or a documented rotary feed rule); it's meant for flat and bull-nose end mills. It finds each layer's areas on a grid at its sample spacing (the tool is dropped exactly at every point of the path, and a move whose middle would dip into the model is split, but stock standing between two samples can be left for the next layer or the finish), and it follows the stock it leaves on a grid of the same kind, so a finish after it starts from that with a small margin. A finish that starts from a roughing still cuts in layers where anything it left is thicker than the pass depth, following the whole pattern. The tool always points at the axis (no ball-nose offset in Y), the reach check takes the deepest cut against the stickout rather than modelling the collet nut, and undercuts are reported but not shaded on the model. Only an A axis along X is supported. Turn times use the machine's turn speed, or a nominal 90° a second until it's set, and 30 seconds for each turn by hand; none has been timed on a real machine. Wrapped cuts are timed by the tool tip's path. Rounding, wrapped 2.5D toolpaths and 3D rotary roughing and finishing all slow their feed where A would otherwise have to turn faster than the turn speed (and the chip-load check warns when that thins the chip below the chart); until it's set that's the nominal 90° a second, so on a slower axis the controller slows them further and they take longer than estimated. Avid's rotary hasn't been timed, so the rotary demos leave the turn speed unset and open with a chip-load warning near the axis that says it comes from the assumed speed; a Mach4 `Machine.ini` import reads the real one from the A motor. A's acceleration isn't modelled (the import shows it but doesn't use it). With a flat end mill, 3D rotary finishing's spirals and rings add a turn at the foot of each shoulder where the tip jumps by more than the pass depth from one turn or ring to the next; a smaller step, or a steep slope, can still leave up to a stepover of material at its foot. A wrapped wall cut straight along the blank shows very slightly radial in the simulation (it keeps one surface height per angle). Rotary projects are saved as format 3, format 4 when the blank is turned by hand, format 5 when designs wrap around it or it has a spiral or a rounding toolpath, format 6 when a V-carve has a start depth or is an inlay plug, format 7 with a 3D model on the blank or a 3D rotary finish, format 8 with a chamfer, fluting or prism toolpath, and format 12 with a 3D rotary roughing. Imported G-code with A words is simulated as if the part never turned, with a warning; its `G93` moves are timed by their F words. Not yet checked on Avid's rotary: which way positive A turns, and whether its M6 macro in rotary mode re-zeroes Z on the axis.
- V-carve inlays ([how to](using-nativecam.md#v-carve-inlays)) are two V-carve toolpaths, a pocket and a plug, rather than one operation that makes both; the plug stays where the design is, so cut the two sides one at a time. The plug is always mirrored left to right, and its boundary is a selected vector or a margin round the design (for the whole board, draw a rectangle the size of the board). A V-bit with a flat tip leaves narrow parts of the plug taller than the pocket's grooves; Checks warns, but the plug isn't adjusted for it. When the V-bit clears flat areas itself it cuts offset rings only, which is slow over large areas. Projects with a plug or a V-carve start depth are saved as format 6, which builds from before inlays refuse to open. No inlay pair has been cut on a real machine yet.
- Chamfers, flutes and prisms ([how to](using-nativecam.md#chamfers-flutes-and-prisms)) haven't been cut on a real machine yet. Chamfers and prisms need a V-bit or engraving bit: there's no angled chamfer with a round-nose bit, and no **Overcut** (Vectric's offset that keeps a V-bit's tip off the bevel). A top-edge chamfer leaves the bevel uncut where shapes are closer together, or narrower, than twice its width, rather than carving the narrow part as a V-carve would. A prism's bit cuts past the outline; the area round it isn't cleared for you. Flutes follow the material top (or a start depth): they aren't projected onto a 3D model. Projects with any of them are saved as format 8, which older builds refuse to open.
- Tapping and boring cycles in imported G-code are simulated only for controllers whose documentation describes them (Avid Mach4, Centroid and LinuxCNC; see [Supported files](using-nativecam.md#supported-files)); for others, and for a cycle a controller means something else by (`G76` threads on LinuxCNC), they're skipped with a warning and their holes don't show. A back bore (`G87`) cuts under the material, which the simulation can't show, so it's carved as a hole right through, the width of the tool. Taps are cut in one pass: a `Q` word isn't read as a peck depth. The spindle reversing at the bottom of a tap, orienting for `G76` and `G87`, and a `G88` stop to retract by hand are timed at 0.5 s, 0.5 s and 10 s; on a machine that can't orient its spindle, Mach4 stops for you to do it by hand.
- Toolpath templates ([how to](using-nativecam.md#templates)) find vectors by layer or by kind, not by size (Vectric's circle-diameter filter), and there's no "create the missing layers" step: a toolpath whose layer is missing is added empty. DXF, SVG, PDF and Illustrator layers are read on import (an SVG's from Inkscape layers or the named groups straight under its root); a DXF layer that's turned off or frozen is imported like any other. A toolpath bound to a layer or kind picks up new vectors on Recalculate All, on a layer change and on import, but not when a shape is drawn with **Add Shape** until you recalculate. The job setup a template carries leaves out the material's width and height and any rotary blank (a rotary template's index angles are kept). On a rotary job the sheet's thickness is the blank's radius, so a **Through** depth there reaches past the axis, as a new profile's depth always has.
- Tiling ([how to](using-nativecam.md#jobs-bigger-than-the-bed)) hasn't been cut on a real machine yet. Registration holes are drilled for feed-through tiling only, two at a time. Where a profile breaks at a tile line the bit comes back onto the wall with a plunge or a ramp rather than a lead. Clamps are checked where they're drawn on the design, which moves with the material. Near tile lines, start depths and leads don't count on earlier toolpaths' cuts (the tiles cut in a different order there), so a toolpath can cut some air there. The run-time estimate leaves out moving the material between tiles.

Bug reports, results from your own machine and code reviews are all very welcome.
