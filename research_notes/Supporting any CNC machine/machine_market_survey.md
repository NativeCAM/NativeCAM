# Hobby and prosumer CNC router market survey: specs for a NativeCAM starter machine library (as of Nov 2026)

How to read this file:
- Units are given as the source publishes them. Conversions I computed are marked "(conv.)".
- **[f]** means I fetched and read the page or file myself. **[s]** means the fact comes from a web-search result summary of that URL and I did not open the page. Treat [s] items as lower confidence.
- "n/p" means not published, or not found in the sources I reached. Nothing in the tables is estimated unless it is marked "est." or "inferred".
- Acceleration is almost never on spec sheets. Where a number exists it comes from firmware defaults, vendor forum posts or community `$$` dumps, and each one is labelled with where it came from.

## Q1. Avid CNC (NativeCAM's current default): confirm PRO4848, PRO4896, PRO6060, PRO2448 and Benchtop PRO specs

### Takeaway
The PRO-series work areas and the 250/400/600 IPM cut and 500/800/1200 IPM rapid tiers are confirmed on Avid's own pages. The big change is the controller. Since about December 2024, Avid's standard controller is the **"EX" control system on Centroid CNC12** (Ethernet motion control), not Mach4 + Ethernet SmoothStepper. Mach4 is now the legacy install base. NativeCAM's Mach4-specific assumptions (G0 exact stop, the 50 in/s² default, Mach4 CV planner) only fit older machines. There is no current Avid model called "PRO2448" or "Benchtop Pro2". The 2x4 PRO is the **PRO4824**, and the benchtops are the **Benchtop PRO 2424/2436** and **Benchtop Standard 2424/2436**.

### Cited Findings
- **PRO4896 machine page [f]:** X 49-1/2" (1260 mm), Y 98-3/4" (2508 mm), Z 8" (203 mm) standard or 12" (304 mm) option. NEMA 23: 250 IPM cut / 500 IPM rapid. NEMA 34: 400 / 800. ClearPath servo: 600 / 1200. Repeatability ±0.002" (0.05 mm), accuracy ±0.005" (0.127 mm). The controller is listed as the "Avid CNC EX control system" with "CNC12 Control Software (Laptop or PC not included)". Spindles: 4 HP or 8.7 HP Hiteco with VFD (manual tool change), or AV40S (4 HP) / AV70S (7 HP) "Smart Spindle", which is ATC-capable. The "Smart ATC System + 10 Tool Holders" is a +$3,084.70 option. Base price $11,389. No spindle RPM range is printed. — [Avid PRO4896 machine](https://www.avidcnc.com/pro4896-4-x-8-cnc-router-machine-p-1337.html)
- **PRO4848 [s]:** X and Y 49-1/2" (1260 mm), Z 8" (203 mm) to 12" (304 mm). Rapids 500 / 800 / 1200 IPM by drive option. — [Avid PRO4848 machine](https://www.avidcnc.com/pro4848-4-x-4-cnc-router-machine-p-1335.html)
- **PRO6060 (from the earlier NativeCAM research note, which fetched Avid's page):** work area 61-3/8" × 61-3/8" × 8" Z (12" optional). Same 250/400/600 cut and 500/800/1200 rapid table. Rack-and-pinion X/Y, ballscrew Z. — [Avid PRO6060 kit](https://www.avidcnc.com/pro6060-5-x-5-cnc-router-kit-p-634.html); summarised in `research_notes/Toolpath optimization for Avid CNC/mach4_motion_cycle_time.md`
- **PRO4824 (2'×4') [s, third-party database]:** 1220 × 610 × 152 mm work area. I found no Avid page for it, so its rapid tier is unconfirmed. "PRO2448" returned no results. — [cncrouterinfo PRO4824](https://cncrouterinfo.pages.dev/routers/avid-pro4824)
- **PRO60120 (5'×10'):** appears only in a forum sale listing. No specs found. — [Avid forum (search result)](https://forum.avidcnc.com/latest?page=1)
- **Benchtop PRO 2436 [s]:** X 26" (660 mm), Y 37.84" (961 mm), Z 6" (152 mm) or 9" (230 mm) depending on crossmember position. Rapids 300 / 400 / 500 IPM and cut 200 / 250 / 350 IPM (three drive options). Spec PDFs exist for BTP2424 and BTP2436. — [Avid Benchtop PRO 2436](https://www.avidcnc.com/benchtop-pro-2436-2-x-3-cnc-machine-p-1333.html); [BTP2436 spec PDF](https://www.avidcnc.com/dl/cad.pdf/BTP2436_Specs.pdf); [BTP2424 spec PDF](https://www.avidcnc.com/dl/cad.pdf/BTP2424_Specs.pdf). A third-party site's 610 × 610 mm figure for the 2436 conflicts with Avid's numbers. — [cncrouterinfo Benchtop Pro](https://cncrouterinfo.pages.dev/routers/avid-benchtop-pro)
- **Controller change [f]:** Manufacturing News (Dec 1, 2024) reports Avid "has chosen Centroid CNC technology to power its next generation EX CNC controllers" for its router and plasma lines. It is the standard controller for new kits, a "plug & play upgrade controller for existing Avid CNC customers", and a DIY package. It is paired with Teknic ClearPath servos and has "motion smoothing and auto touch plate set-up routines". — [MfgNewsWeb Dec 2024](https://www.mfgnewsweb.com/archives/4/67461/Controls-dec24/ad_info.aspx)
- CNC12 runs on a Windows PC and talks over Ethernet to Centroid motion hardware. Forum threads cover migrating from Mach4 to EX and EX post-processor problems [s]. — [Avid forum: Migrating from Mach4 to EX](https://forum.avidcnc.com/t/migrating-challenge-from-mach4-to-ex-controller/3891); [Avid forum EX tag](https://forum.avidcnc.com/tag/ex-controller/84)
- **Acceleration:** Avid publishes none. The Mach4 default that owners cite is 50 in/s² X/Y (1270 mm/s²) (from the earlier NativeCAM note). — [Avid forum: Mach4 accel macro](https://forum.avidcnc.com/t/mach4-motor-accel-a-macro-and-post-combo/2505). For CNC12, one user says the settings "are default values when selecting the avidPRO layout", but the values are not posted [s]. — [Avid forum migrating thread](https://forum.avidcnc.com/t/migrating-challenge-from-mach4-to-ex-controller/3891)
- **Posts:** an "Avid EX" post is built into VCarve (11+). Avid also hosts Vectric downloads for EX/CNC12 and Mach4 [s]. The Fusion library has a generic "Avid CNC" milling post with laser support [s]. A community Fusion post exists for Centroid CNC12 and needs CNC12 4.16+ [s]. — [Avid forum RhinoCAM/Fusion posts](https://forum.avidcnc.com/t/rhinocam-fusion-360-post-processors/4351); [corbinstreehouse: Avid ATC Vectric post](https://www.corbinstreehouse.com/blog/2023/05/avid-cnc-atc-post-processor-setup-vectric-vcarve-vectric-aspire); [Autodesk Post Library](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/Fusion-CAM/files/MFG-POST-PROCESSOR-LIBRARY-REF.htm); [swissi2000 Fusion post for Centroid](https://swissi2000.github.io/Fusion-360-Milling-Post-Processor-for-Centroid)

### Inferences
- NativeCAM's `MachineProfile.avidPRO6060` (800 IPM rapid, 50 in/s², NEMA 34 tier) still fits a Mach4-era PRO6060 with NEMA 34. A new Avid order, though, ships CNC12 and often ClearPath, which is the 600/1200 tier. NativeCAM probably needs a **controller dialect** field (Mach4 vs Centroid CNC12) that is separate from the machine frame. CNC12 has its own G-code dialect, macros and M6 handling, and a different planner, so the "Mach4 runs G0 in exact stop" comment in `CycleTimeEstimator.swift` should not be applied to EX machines without checking.
- Library entries for Avid: PRO4824, PRO4848, PRO4896, PRO6060, PRO60120 (travel n/p), Benchtop PRO 2424/2436, Benchtop Standard 2424/2436. Each takes a drive tier (N23 / N34 / ClearPath) that sets cut and rapid limits, and a controller (Mach4-ESS legacy / EX-CNC12).

### Gaps
- No Avid-published RPM range for the Hiteco or Smart spindles.
- No CNC12/EX default acceleration or "motion smoothing" parameters are public.
- No official PRO4824 or PRO60120 spec page was reached. Benchtop Standard specs were not retrieved.
- The exact cut-over date to EX as the only option, and whether Mach4 kits are still sold, were not confirmed.

## Q2. Starter machine library table: 37 current or common models

### Takeaway
The table below covers 37 rows across 21 vendors (generic Chinese routers counted as one). Published data is reliable for work area and spindle, patchy for max feed and rapid, and almost absent for acceleration. Where a machine runs open firmware (GRBL, grblHAL), the shipped firmware defaults (Q5) are the best acceleration source. Proprietary controllers (DSP handhelds, WinCNC, ShopBot, Masso, Redline, PathPilot) rarely publish any.

## Q3. Which post processor do Vectric / Fusion / Carbide Create users select, and which vendors publish their own posts or machine files?

### Takeaway
Most hobby vendors now ship their machines *inside Vectric's machine database* (VCarve/Aspire 11+). Onefinity, Carbide 3D (Shapeoko) and Avid (EX) have confirmed entries. GRBL-family machines without their own entry use Vectric's generic "Grbl (mm/inch)" post. In Fusion's library, the relevant entries are "Carbide 3D (Grbl)", "Grbl" and "Avid CNC". Onefinity users pick the generic 3-axis router machine. Vectric 12.5 now stores **machine acceleration** in Machine Configuration for run-time estimates, and Onefinity publishes the values to type in. That is the same idea NativeCAM uses.

## Q4. Which controllers dominate by number of users, and what popularity signals exist?

### Takeaway
No current, rigorous market-share survey exists. All the indirect signals say the **GRBL family (grbl, grblHAL, FluidNC, Carbide's Grbl fork) is the largest user base by unit count** in hobby routers. It runs Shapeoko, X-Carve, LongMill, AltMill, BobsCNC, Genmitsu, FoxAlien, TwoTrees and most 3018-class machines. **Mach3/Mach4** has a large legacy base, especially Chinese routers and older Avid. **DSP handhelds** (RichAuto) dominate Chinese and Axiom/Laguna pro-sumer machines. **Centroid** is growing (Avid EX, CAMaster). WinCNC (ShopSabre), ShopBot, PathPilot, UCCNC, Masso, Buildbotics and Redline are each tied to one vendor or niche. The only published share numbers (CNCCookbook) are about ten years old and predate GRBL's rise.

### Cited Findings
- **CNCCookbook control survey (≈2012, n = 200) [f]:** hobby controls Mach3 66%, LinuxCNC 25%. Pro: Fanuc 26%, then Haas, Heidenhain, Centroid, Siemens. — [CNCCookbook control market shares](https://www.cnccookbook.com/cnc-control-market-shares-what-are-the-most-popular-controls)
- **Later CNCCookbook surveys [s]:** Mach3 at 47% of low-end controls (2017). "GRBL, CNC USB, Mach 4, and FlashCut have also taken bites out of Mach 3". Tormach's switch to PathPilot/LinuxCNC had a big effect. The surveys were skewed by a LinuxCNC forum link. — [CNCCookbook 2017 survey](https://www.cnccookbook.com/choose-best-cnc-control-2017-cnc-control-survey-results)
- **Fusion:** Grbl is "one of our most widely used posts on the milling side" [s]. — [Autodesk Fusion Grbl post blog](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/fusion-360-grbl-post/)
- **GitHub activity (queried Nov 19, 2026) [f]:** gnea/grbl 4,587 stars (last push Jun 2024, so effectively frozen); bdring/FluidNC 2,649 stars (active); LinuxCNC 2,542; cncjs 2,670; Smoothieware 1,416; grblHAL/core 531 (active); Sienci gSender 390; OneFinityCNC/onefinity-firmware 24. — GitHub API: [gnea/grbl](https://github.com/gnea/grbl), [FluidNC](https://github.com/bdring/FluidNC), [grblHAL core](https://github.com/grblHAL/core), [LinuxCNC](https://github.com/LinuxCNC/linuxcnc), [gSender](https://github.com/Sienci-Labs/gsender)

- **Controller migrations:** Avid moved from Mach4 to Centroid (2024) [f] ([MfgNewsWeb](https://www.mfgnewsweb.com/archives/4/67461/Controls-dec24/ad_info.aspx)). CAMaster moved from WinCNC to Centroid [s] ([Sawmill Creek](https://sawmillcreek.org/goto/post?id=3287071)). - Masso markets the G3 as a PC-less controller ("without the headaches of a PC-based controller") from AU $851.62 [s]. — [Masso G3](https://www.masso.com.au/product/masso-g3-cnc-controller/)

### Inferences
- By installed units: GRBL family (including Carbide Motion and grblHAL) > Mach3/Mach4 > DSP handhelds (RichAuto/NK) > everything else. Among *new* prosumer 4×4 and 4×8 machines, closed-loop and Ethernet controllers (Centroid, Masso, Redline, ClearPath servos) are clearly growing. This is my judgement from the signals above, not a measured share.
- For NativeCAM, a high-quality **Grbl/grblHAL** post plus estimator model covers most hobby users. **Mach4**, **Centroid CNC12** and **Masso** are the next most valuable dialects because they cover the larger 4×4 and 4×8 machines (Avid, Onefinity Elite Gen 1, CAMaster, Chinese retrofits).

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) survey of controller share was found. Vendor unit sales (Carbide 3D, Inventables, Onefinity) are not published.
- Community size metrics (forum members, Facebook and Reddit group sizes) were not collected.

## Q5. Typical shipped acceleration, max-rate and junction/corner settings (estimator defaults)

### Takeaway
Shipped X/Y accelerations cluster around **400–750 mm/s²** for belt and lead-screw hobby machines, and **1,270–1,500 mm/s²** for ball-screw or rack prosumer frames (Avid Mach4, AltMill). Onefinity's official values are 500–750 mm/s². Z is usually lower. GRBL junction deviation ships at **0.01–0.02 mm**. Max rates range from about 4,000 mm/min (LongMill MK2) to 10,000–15,000 mm/min (Shapeoko 4, BobsCNC, AltMill), and 25,000 mm/min on the AltMill 4×8 Y.

Other related facts:
- Grbl's `$11` junction deviation is the corner-speed control. Upstream profiles use 0.01–0.02 mm. FluidNC exposes per-axis `$axes/x/acceleration` [s]. — [grbl defaults.h](https://github.com/gnea/grbl/blob/master/grbl/defaults.h); [FluidNC fork](https://github.com/sle118/FluidNC)
- Sienci warns that "default acceleration settings, especially on the Z-axis" caused false closed-loop alarms on early AltMills [s]. — [Sienci AltMill search result](https://sienci.com/altmill)
- A Machsupport moderator calls 400 mm/s² "very underpowered" for Mach4 and recommends "at least 1000-1500mm/sec/sec" (from the earlier NativeCAM note). — [Machsupport](https://www.machsupport.com/forum/index.php?topic=38272.0)

### Inferences
- Suggested estimator defaults by class, for when a machine has no better data (my synthesis of the table above, mm/s² X/Y / Z):
  - Belt or V-wheel GRBL (Shapeoko 3/4, X-Carve, LongMill): **500 / 300**, JD **0.02 mm**, rapid **5000–10000 mm/min** XY and **1000–3000** Z.
  - Ball-screw desktop GRBL (Shapeoko 5 Pro, Genmitsu XL, BobsCNC): **500 / 300**, JD 0.01. Rapid limited to about **5000 mm/min** by step rate, per users.
  - Closed-loop grblHAL prosumer (AltMill): **1500 / 1500**, JD 0.01, rapid 15000 (25000 on long Y).
  - Onefinity: **750** (Buildbotics) or **500** (Masso), rapid 400 / 600 ipm.
  - Avid Mach4: **1270** (50 in/s²) XY. Z is unknown, so keep NativeCAM's 25 in/s² placeholder.
  - DSP / WinCNC / ShopBot / Centroid machines: no published acceleration. Use the published rapid as the velocity cap and a conservative **≈500–1000 mm/s²** until the user enters real values. This is an estimate and should be labelled as one in the UI.
- Onefinity's official values convert to round metric numbers (500 and 750 mm/s²). That suggests they were copied from the controllers' metric configs. NativeCAM can show both units without losing precision.
- Offering a "paste your `$$` output" importer would fill acceleration, max-rate, travel and JD fields for every GRBL-family machine. That covers most of the user base (Q4).

### Gaps
- No official Carbide 3D table of shipped $110/$120/$11 per model. The SO4 XXL values are one user's machine.
- No published acceleration for Centroid CNC12 (Avid EX, CAMaster), Masso beyond Onefinity's number, Redline/RTS-2, RichAuto DSP, WinCNC, ShopBot, PathPilot (24R), UCCNC (Stepcraft) or Makera.
- Onefinity Gen 2 Elite acceleration and jerk were not found.
- The FluidNC config YAMLs that vendors ship were not located for any surveyed machine. None of the surveyed vendors appears to ship FluidNC as standard; the TwoTrees TTC450 runs GRBL_ESP32, FluidNC's predecessor.
